# Automatic portfolio diversification and historical correlation

The existing authenticated portfolio review now reports portfolio-level exposure alongside per-holding research. It uses recorded market values from owner-local snapshots, exact equity ISINs and exact registered AMFI scheme codes. Duplicate resolved positions aggregate by identity. Unpriced holdings and unresolved identities are counted separately. Unknown or conflicting company sector classifications remain unknown; direct-equity weights use the total priced direct-equity denominator, and mutual-fund category weights use the total priced fund denominator. AMFI category metadata does not require a complete NAV history.

Illustrative review prompts use 25% per resolved identity, 60% across the top three resolved identities and 40% of direct-equity value for a known sector. These are review triggers, not personalized targets or optimal allocations. Top-three resolved exposure is a lower-bound observation when identities or prices are missing. No fund holdings look-through or underlying overlap is inferred from scheme categories.

The existing scheduled owner review also computes Pearson correlations for up to 12 largest resolved priced direct-equity identities using locally stored validated, adjusted closes within the preceding 90 calendar days. The series must have one known source, no duplicate dates, positive prices and recent observations. Only exactly matched consecutive observed return intervals are compared (intervals longer than seven days are excluded); at least 30 matched intervals and a recent common endpoint are required. Constant returns and raw, mixed or incomplete evidence are rejected. A correlation of at least 0.8 is labeled for review, not a prediction. No missing prices are imputed; no fund correlation, portfolio covariance or opportunity ranking is inferred.

The existing Portfolio Doctor automatically presents the summary, review prompts, classification coverage and correlation details without manual scans. No new API calls, AI providers, paid services, trade execution or database migrations are introduced. Existing saved snapshots may lack the new fields until their next refresh. Live acceptance requires suitable adjusted price history and verification of sector metadata. All calculations are descriptive, not a personalized asset-allocation recommendation.
